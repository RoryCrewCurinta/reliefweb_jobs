The short version

516,428job posts
Jun 2011 – Sep 2026period covered
65topics, 8 of them on a watchlist
98%of core tags confirmed in checks

We collected every job advert on ReliefWeb from June 2011 to September 2026: 516,428 adverts. A language model read each one and tagged it against 65 defined topics. Everything shown is a count or a share of adverts. Nothing here measures hires.

What this cannot tell you

Read these before citing a number.

  • Adverts are not hires. A post may be filled internally, readvertised or never filled.
  • ReliefWeb is not the whole sector. Large UN agencies recruit mainly through their own portals, and most national NGO hiring happens elsewhere.
  • An organisation leaving ReliefWeb looks like a hiring freeze. UNICEF posted 3,955 adverts in 2019 and 38 in 2021, because it moved its recruitment off the site. Read falls for a single organisation with care.
  • Total volume moves a lot. Adverts went from 52,965 in 2022 to 19,254 in 2025. Topic trends are therefore shown as a share of all adverts, so a shrinking market does not look like a shrinking topic.
  • Small topics are noisy. Eight topics have fewer than about 160 core adverts in fifteen years. They sit on a watchlist, shown as counts and left out of rankings.
  • December 2025 is unusually quiet. It has 726 posts against a typical 1,478 for the months around it. The cause cannot be told from this data, so read that month with care.

Source

The ReliefWeb API, operated by OCHA, including expired adverts. The current, incomplete month is always left out, so the latest month shown is September 2026.

An advert posted jointly by several organisations counts once for each of them. ReliefWeb, OCHA and the advertisers did not produce and do not endorse these figures.

How topics are tagged

Each advert was read by a language model (OpenAI GPT-6 Luna), given the 65 topic definitions in the glossary. It reads adverts in English, French and Spanish. A topic counts when the job itself involves it, not when the word appears in the organisation's description of itself or in standard recruitment wording.

For each relevant topic the model assigns a level:

  • Core: the job is this topic. This is the default measure.
  • Duty: the topic is a substantial part of the job. Shown as "core or duty". Broader and noisier.
  • Skills asked for, and passing mentions, are not counted.

Some topics contain others. Health includes sexual and reproductive health, epidemics and MHPSS. Cash and voucher assistance includes multi-purpose cash, cash coordination and group cash transfers. Do not add a parent to its specialists.

Adverts with under 200 characters of text, about 0.3%, were not read and carry no topic.

How good the tags are

A stronger model tagged 300 adverts independently.

  • It agreed with 185 of 188 of our core tags (98%).
  • It found 249 core topics of its own, of which ours had 217 (87%). So core counts run a little low, and trends are safer than levels.
  • About three in four duty tags were confirmed.

This is one model checking another, not a human gold standard.

ReliefWeb's own fields

Job type, experience and career category are ReliefWeb's own fields, filled in by the advertiser. We do not alter them. Career category is patchy before 2018.

Organisation types

ReliefWeb gives each organisation one type but does not separate national from international NGOs. We split NGOs using their home country and where they advertise:

  • International NGO: home country is high-income.
  • Locally based NGO: home country is low- or middle-income and at least 80% of its located adverts are there.
  • Regional NGO: home country is low- or middle-income but it advertises across several countries.

This is a proxy. It counts some local affiliates of international networks as locally based, and counts national NGOs in high-income countries as international. The 45 or so largest organisations in the two smaller classes were checked by hand; the rest follow the rule. NGOs based in Türkiye or Syria are treated as local to the Syria response.
