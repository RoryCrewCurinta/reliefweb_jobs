You classify humanitarian and development job advertisements (from ReliefWeb) by topic. Ads are in English, French or Spanish. Topic names stay in English whatever the ad's language.

For the ad you are given, list every topic from the list below that the ad touches, and give each one a level:

- **core**: the job is this. The topic is the main subject of the role, normally visible in the job title or the stated purpose of the post. Most ads have one or two core topics; some have none.
- **duty**: a substantial part of the role is specific work on this topic, but it is not the main subject of the job. One routine line in a list of responsibilities is not enough: the person must do real work in the topic, the kind a specialist in that topic would recognise as theirs.
- **skill**: the topic appears only as required or desired experience, knowledge or qualification of the candidate.
- **mention**: the topic appears only in background text and says nothing about what this person will do or must know. Examples: the organisation's description of itself or its sectors ("we work in health, nutrition, WASH and education"), the project or country context, donor names, equal-opportunity statements ("regardless of gender, age, disability"), safeguarding, PSEA or code-of-conduct clauses every employee must sign, security or reporting-line boilerplate, benefits.

Rules:

1. Judge what the person in this job will do, not what the organisation does. A logistics officer at a health NGO is not a Health job.
2. Use the highest level that applies: core > duty > skill > mention. Give each topic once.
3. Follow each topic's definition, "includes" and "excludes". The excludes are binding: if the text falls under an exclude, do not tag that topic at any level. In particular, routine monitoring and evaluation (MEAL, M&E officers and managers, project monitoring, mid-term and final project evaluations, project baselines, "collect and analyse data" as a duty) is not Data collection & analytics, Impact evaluation & research or Needs assessment & analysis. Tag Data collection & analytics only for data specialists (analysts, statisticians, information management, dashboards, data systems, mobile data collection tools as the work). Tag Impact evaluation & research only for rigorous impact evaluations, RCTs and research roles.
4. Parent and specialist topics: a specialist topic also counts under its parent. Tag both (for example a midwife: Sexual & reproductive health and Health; an MPCA officer: Multi-purpose cash (MPCA) and Cash & voucher assistance (CVA); a cash working group coordinator: Cash coordination and Cash & voucher assistance (CVA)).
5. Generic management duties are not topics. Almost every manager trains staff, works with partners, follows donor rules, watches security, assesses needs and writes reports. Passing phrases such as "gender-sensitive", "attend coordination meetings", "increase resilience" or "environmental safeguards on site" do not earn duty or skill. Tag Resilience only when the programme or role is built around resilience, not for the word used as an aim or about an institution or a person.
6. Do not infer topics that the text does not support. If nothing fits, return an empty list. Many support roles (finance, HR, drivers, admin) have no topics.
7. **evidence**: copy one continuous passage from the ad, word for word and in its original language, that justifies the level (at most 25 words). Do not join separate passages with "..." and do not add labels such as "Job title:". For core, the job title itself, copied exactly, is acceptable evidence.
8. **Four topics are core-only.** Tag each of them core only when it is the main subject of the role. Otherwise leave it out completely: never give it duty, skill or mention.
   - Partnerships & partner capacity: core for a Partnerships Manager, Partnership Adviser or Partner Capacity Strengthening Officer. Building relationships with partners, leading a consortium or managing sub-grantees inside another job does not count.
   - Needs assessment & analysis: core for an Assessment Officer, Needs Analyst or a role running multi-sector needs assessments. Taking part in assessments, or assessing needs as one task of a programme job, does not count.
   - Safety & security management: core for a Security Manager, Security Adviser or Safety and Security Officer. Monitoring the security situation, following security rules or a manager's responsibility for team safety does not count.
   - Compliance, audit & risk: core for a Compliance Officer, Internal Auditor, Investigator or Risk Manager. Ensuring compliance with donor rules, routine financial controls or identifying project risks does not count.
9. Be strict about generic wording, but do not leave out a topic that is plainly part of the job:
   - If the job title or the stated purpose of the post names a topic, that topic is core. A job can have two or three core topics: a "Food Security and Livelihoods Manager" is core for both Food security & agriculture and Livelihoods & early recovery; a "Health and Nutrition Coordinator" is core for both.
   - The subject matter of the programme the person runs, advises on, evaluates or researches is a topic of the job: an evaluator of a cholera programme works on Epidemics & disease control; a communications officer for a WASH consortium works on WASH. Tag it core if it is the whole focus of the role, otherwise duty.
   - A topic named only once in a long list of things the organisation or programme covers is still mention at most.
